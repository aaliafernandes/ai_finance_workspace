# Lab 11 — Part D: Question and Partner Exchange 1

**Company:** Apple Inc. (AAPL)  
**Purpose:** Educational finance coursework only; not personalized investment advice.

## My question

**Which assumptions drive Apple’s forecast and value, and what explains their effects?**

Apple’s forecast is affected by total revenue growth, including product demand
and Services growth, and by operating margin. Higher revenue growth increases
sales. If operating margin is held constant, higher sales increase operating
profit and free cash flow. Operating margin matters because it determines how
much operating profit remains from each dollar of revenue. Services mix can
matter because Services has historically carried a higher gross-margin
percentage than Products.

## Partner Exchange 1

My partner’s company is Abercrombie & Fitch. My partner identified revenue
growth as an important input. In my own words, higher apparel sales can increase
revenue; if costs do not rise at the same rate, gross profit, operating profit,
and free cash flow can also increase.

My partner and I agreed that both Apple and Abercrombie & Fitch depend on
sales, so revenue growth can affect revenue, profit, cash flow, and value. We
also questioned whether revenue growth is the only important assumption. It is
not: Apple’s operating margin and Services mix can also affect the share of
revenue that becomes profit and cash flow. For Abercrombie & Fitch, gross
margin, pricing, inventory costs, and operating expenses can also matter.

**Question I asked my partner:** What historical revenue growth, same-store
sales trend, store-growth plan, or management guidance supports the lower,
base, and higher revenue-growth range?

**Question for my Apple ranges:** What historical growth and margin evidence
supports my lower, base, and higher Apple assumption paths?

**Gap identified before using AI:** We needed evidence to support the lower,
base, and higher ranges rather than assuming sales growth alone determines
value.
