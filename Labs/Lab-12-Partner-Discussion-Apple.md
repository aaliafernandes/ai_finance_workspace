## I — Presentation of Apple analysis

### 1. Target selection

Apple has a large hardware ecosystem and a growing recurring Services business. It earns revenue from iPhone, Mac, iPad, wearables, and accessories, plus Services including the App Store, cloud, AppleCare, subscriptions, payments, and advertising.

My initial view was **Watch — defer initiation**. Apple is a strong company, but I needed to determine whether its expected cash flows and valuation supported the market price.

### 2. Company and evidence

My primary source was Apple's FY2025 Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended September 27, 2025, filed October 31, 2025. Amounts are in U.S. dollars and mostly reported in millions.

| Evidence | FY2025 result |
|---|---:|
| Total net sales | $416,161M; +6% |
| Services sales | $109,158M; +14% |
| Services gross margin | 75.4% |
| Products gross margin | 36.8% |
| Operating cash flow | $111,482M |
| Capital expenditures | $12,715M |
| GAAP diluted EPS | $7.46 |

Services is important because it grew faster than total sales and has a much higher margin than Products. A growing Services mix could support Apple's overall profitability. Services growth could weaken if regulation, competition, or customer demand changes.

### 3. Pro-forma

I built a five-year operating forecast using total revenue growth and operating margin as the two main independent assumptions.

| Assumption | Base case |
|---|---|
| Revenue growth, Year 1 | 6% |
| Revenue growth, Year 2 | 5% |
| Revenue growth, Year 3 | 4% |
| Revenue growth, Year 4 | 3% |
| Revenue growth, Year 5 | 3% |
| Operating margin, each forecast year | 32% |

The revenue-growth path starts with Apple's reported FY2025 growth and tapers because Apple is already very large. The 32% margin is a rounded historical reference based on FY2025 operating income divided by revenue.

The model links:

`Revenue growth → forecast revenue → operating profit → FCFF proxy`

The FCFF proxy uses FY2025 operating cash flow less capital expenditures. It is not fully defined FCFF because the cash-interest-paid adjustment remains unresolved. Therefore, I use it to analyze operating sensitivity, not as a final per-share valuation.

### 4. Valuation

I used a DCF and a peer P/E comparison.

The saved DCF output was **negative $4.08 per diluted share**, but I do not treat that as Apple's fair value. The model used a $100M starting-FCFF training placeholder and a 10% WACC training placeholder. The starting FCFF is far too small compared with Apple's reported cash flow, creating a scale mismatch.

For the peer P/E analysis, I used Microsoft and Alphabet as qualified references. They meet the policy for scale, profitability, platform economics, and Services exposure, but they are not direct Apple substitutes:

- Microsoft has more enterprise software and cloud exposure.
- Alphabet has much more advertising exposure.
- Apple has a more hardware-led ecosystem with Services attached.

Using their P/E multiples, Apple's implied reference range was **$204.66 to $229.32 per share**, with a median of **$216.99**. Apple's September 10, 2026 market close was **$326.57**. I do not average the P/E range with the DCF because the DCF is not currently usable.

### 5. Sensitivity and drivers

I tested revenue growth and operating margin one at a time.

| Driver | Lower | Base | Higher |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue growth, Years 1–5 | 4%, 3%, 2%, 1%, 1% | 6%, 5%, 4%, 3%, 3% | 8%, 7%, 6%, 5%, 5% |
| Operating margin, Years 1–5 | 30% | 32% | 34% |

Over the selected ranges, revenue growth had the larger final-year impact.

| Driver | Operating-profit span | FCFF-proxy span |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue growth | $31,416.0M | $23,321.0M |
| Operating margin | $20,442.0M | $15,174.7M |

In the higher revenue-growth case, final-year revenue rose from $511,051.1M to $562,022.1M. With margin held at 32%, operating profit increased by $16,310.7M and the FCFF proxy increased by $12,107.9M.

The causal link is higher sales → more operating profit → more FCFF proxy. This result shows that revenue growth was the larger driver over the selected ranges; it does not establish that revenue growth is always more important than operating margin.

### 6. Interpretation

My conclusion remains **Watch — defer initiation**. Apple's operating evidence is strong, especially Services growth and margin. Revenue growth was the largest tested operating driver. The peer P/E range is below the saved market price, but the peers have different economic models, so it is only a reference range.

The biggest limitation is that the DCF has not been rebuilt with fully sourced, consistently scaled FCFF and a dated WACC. Until that is completed, I cannot rely on a per-share DCF valuation.

The next research priorities are:

- Build a fully sourced starting FCFF, including the cash-interest-paid treatment.
- Estimate a dated WACC using a risk-free rate, beta, cost of debt, tax rate, and capital structure.
- Investigate Services durability and regulatory pressure.
- Investigate product demand and Greater China performance.
- Test a more directly comparable hardware-and-services peer, if one is available.

## E — Explain back and feedback

### Partner's explanation back of Apple analysis

**Strength:** The analysis clearly linked Apple's installed base and Services growth to the revenue and margin assumptions. Apple's FY2025 Form 10-K reports that Services revenue grew **14% to $109.158B**, compared with **6% total company sales growth**, and that Services had a **75.4% gross margin**. This supports why Services can matter disproportionately to Apple's profitability compared with hardware alone.

**Improvement:** Revisit the **3% terminal-growth assumption** after the explicit forecast period. Apple's installed base and Services revenue may support durable cash flows, but they should not be treated as growing indefinitely at recent Services growth rates. Test a lower terminal-growth case after rebuilding the DCF with consistently scaled FCFF and a dated WACC, or explain why 3% remains reasonable for a mature company of Apple's size.

### My explanation back of my partner's analysis

My understanding is that my partner's conclusion is **Watch — defer initiation**. ### Reflection

A question that made me reconsider my analysis was:

> How do you know your 3% terminal-growth assumption does not treat Apple's installed base and Services growth as permanent?

This helped me understand that the installed base and Services business can support stronger near-term growth and margins, but they do not automatically justify higher perpetual growth. Apple is already a mature, very large company, so terminal growth should remain conservative and be tested against lower cases after the DCF is rebuilt with a valid FCFF starting point and dated WACC.
